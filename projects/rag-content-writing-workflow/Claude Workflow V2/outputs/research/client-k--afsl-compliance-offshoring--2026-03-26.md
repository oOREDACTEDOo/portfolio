**Table stakes — covered by most articles:**

- Due diligence, contracts, monitoring checklist
- Cyber risk and data privacy as primary concerns
- Commissioner Kirkland's "agnostic but governance must be watertight" position

**What the benchmark does well:**
- AIC Solutions: clear compliance checklist (6 items), ASIC commissioner quote
- Clayton Utz: sophisticated framing of 5 concurrent ASIC workstreams, board-level governance angle
- Sophie Grace: specific good practice examples (auditing, documenting)

**Where the benchmark is weak:**
- None address what "good offshore governance" looks like FROM THE PROVIDER SIDE — they all write from the licensee perspective with no practical model
- No article explains the difference between a staff augmentation model (full oversight) vs. a BPO black box (ASIC's actual problem)
- None address fintech specifically (SOCI, API access to consumer financial data, APRA vs. ASIC obligations)
- No article positions a specific offshore partner as part of the compliance solution
- Checklists exist but no framework for building a compliant offshore team structure from day one
- Zero client voice / lived experience — all regulatory/legal commentary

**Beat strategy:**
1. **Make the provider model distinction explicit:** ASIC's concerns are about black-box BPO arrangements with no licensee visibility. A dedicated, staff-augmentation model with Australian management gives licensees the visibility and control ASIC requires — but benchmark articles never explain this.
2. **Fintech-specific context:** Address SOCI Act obligations, Philippine Data Privacy Act alignment, API data access governance — none of the benchmark articles touch these.
3. **Build-from-scratch governance framework:** Not just a checklist — a practical sequence for setting up a compliant offshore team (before hire, at hire, ongoing). Benchmark articles are reactive (what ASIC found wrong); this content is proactive (how to build it right).
4. **Client voice:** client executive quotes ground the article in operational reality, not just regulatory commentary. No benchmark article has this.
5. **Include a comparison table:** ASIC requirement vs. Client K model — make the compliance case concrete and scannable.

- 10 advice licensees reviewed, 300+ representatives using OSPs
- 6 intermediary businesses connecting AU businesses with Philippines, India, Sri Lanka
- One fund manager: 900 offshore enquiries in 30 days; another: 16,500 from 24 countries in 12 months
- Specific failures: no formal outsourcing policies, no offshore-specific IT policies, no auditing of OSP access, no client disclosure policies
- Obligations: Corporations Act s912A — adequate risk management systems, adequate resources, supervisory arrangements

**From Clayton Utz (March 2026) — legal commentary:**
- ASIC's February 2026 Financial Advice Update: 5 concurrent supervisory workstreams
- 20+ enforcement actions Aug 2025–Feb 2026
- "Effectiveness, not just policies" — SMSF review showed policies that existed didn't catch non-compliant advice
- Board-level engagement essential

**From GRC Essentials (December 2025):**
- Corporations Act s912A: general obligations
- APRA Prudential Standard SPS 231: outsourcing must not compromise obligations or hinder ASIC access
- ASIC RG 104: foundation for general obligations including outsourcing

- Key actions: annual reviews, client transparency, cybersecurity controls, documentation

- Commissioner Kirkland quote: "Advice licensees and REs can outsource services but they cannot outsource their fundamental obligations… When licensees neglect their responsibilities, consumers, investors, and financial services businesses can be exposed to harm, such as exposure of personal information through cyber incidents."
