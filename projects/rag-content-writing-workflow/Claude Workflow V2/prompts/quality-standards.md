# Universal Content Quality Standards — V2

These standards apply to all content regardless of client, content type, or topic. They define the baseline for what constitutes publishable quality. Client-specific criteria (from `content_types.quality_criteria`) and voice profiles layer on top.

Derived from: Ryan Law (Ahrefs Director of Content Marketing), hook/intro craft research, H2 craft research, B2B specificity research. Not written top-down — derived from analysis of what makes genuinely great content work.

---

## The Core Test

Before anything else: **does this content make the reader genuinely better informed than they were before reading it?**

If they already knew most of this — it fails.
If they now understand something they didn't — it passes.

Secondary test: **would a busy professional in this industry stop scanning and start reading?**

If the hook doesn't compel that — rewrite the hook before anything else.

---

## 1. Hook & Intro Craft

The hook is not a summary. It is the moment that converts a scanner into a reader.

**What a great hook does:**
- Creates a curiosity gap, surfaces an emotion, or drops into a scene — without revealing the resolution
- Feels specific to the reader's situation, not generic to the topic
- Breaks the expected pattern (professional jargon, "In today's rapidly changing world...")

**Structural rules:**
- Hook: 1–3 lines maximum. Short and punchy.
- After the hook: bridge (connects hook to content) + promise (what the reader will gain) + authority signal (why this source). Maximum 3 sentences combined.
- Total intro before first H2: under 120 words.

**Technique selection — choose one based on topic and audience:**

| Technique | When to use | Example pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **Specific Pain Callout** | Reader has a named, recurring frustration | "Still [problem] after [effort]?" |
| **Pattern Break (Contrarian)** | Dominant industry advice is wrong or outdated | "Stop [common advice]. Here's what actually works." |
| **Small Change, Big Win** | Topic has a high-leverage, counterintuitive insight | "[Small action] changed [metric] by [specific number]." |
| **Vulnerable Truth** | Building trust on a sensitive or complex topic | "We got this wrong for [time period]. Here's what changed." |
| **Mid-Scene Drop** | High-stakes or emotionally resonant topic | Drop directly into a moment without setup. |
| **Mystery Tease** | Topic has a non-obvious mechanism | "There's a [specific thing] that [outcome]. Most people miss it." |

**What a hook is NOT:**
- A definition: "Offshoring is the practice of..."
- A history: "For decades, businesses have..."
- A summary: "In this article, we'll cover..."
- A generic claim: "In today's competitive landscape..."

## 2. H2 & Heading Craft

An H2 is a mini-headline. It either pulls the reader into the section or lets them skip it.

**Engaging H2 vs. label H2:**

| Label (fails) | Engaging (passes) |
|---|---|
| Our Pricing | Affordable Plans That Grow With You |
| Project Management | Streamline Your Workflow in One Place |
| Benefits of Offshoring | Why Australian Teams Save 40–70% With Philippine Developers |
| How It Works | What Your First 30 Days Looks Like |

**Rules:**
- Benefit-focused: tells the reader what they gain by reading the section
- Action-oriented: uses verbs, implies progress
- 5–8 words
- Specific over vague: include a number, outcome, or named concept when possible
- Must deliver on the H1's promise — if the title promises a guide, H2s are the steps
- MECE: each H2 covers distinct ground. No overlap between sections. All H2s together cover the full topic.

**H3 usage:**
- When a section under an H2 covers more than one sub-idea, use H3s
- H3s follow the same rules: benefit-focused, specific, short
- PAA questions from SERP research become H3 candidates — they map directly to real reader searches

## 3. Writing Quality

**Dense, specific language:**
- Use the most precise word available. "Novel" not "something new." "Worldwide" not "on a global scale."
- Replace abstract nouns with concrete ones: "the optimisation of capital allocation" → "investing capital better"
- Active verbs over passive constructions

**No weasel words:**
Replace every instance of the following with specific examples or remove entirely:
- "business outcomes" → name the outcome
- "experts believe" → name the expert and the claim
- "significant results" → state the result and the number
- "various factors" → list the factors
- "some companies" → name one
- "studies show" → name the study, year, and finding
- "in many cases" → state the condition

**Address the obvious objections:**
For any major claim in the piece, anticipate the reader's most likely pushback and address it directly within that section. If the objection is strong enough to be obvious, it's strong enough to deserve a response.

**Open with the most important thing:**
- In the introduction: lead with the most important point, not the background
- In each paragraph: the first sentence states the controlling idea; the rest supports it
- Don't bury the answer

**Don't make hard things sound easy:**
Acknowledge genuine complexity where it exists. Oversimplifying erodes trust with readers who know the topic. "It depends" is an acceptable answer when followed by clear conditions.

**Sentence rhythm:**
Vary length deliberately. Short sentences land. Longer sentences build context, connect related ideas, and give the reader room to absorb what came before. Monotonous sentence length flattens engagement regardless of content quality.

## 4. B2B Specificity

B2B buyers are risk-averse, time-poor, and accountable to others for the decision. Generic claims fail them.

**The specificity test for every B2B claim:**
Does this sentence include: (a) a quantifiable outcome, (b) a named audience, and (c) a specific condition or timeframe?

**Fails:**
- "Offshoring reduces costs significantly"
- "Our clients see better results"
- "We help businesses grow"

**Passes:**
- "A five-person mid-level development team costs ~$690k/year onshore vs ~$270k offshore — a $420k annual saving"
- "Australian SMBs with 10–50 staff typically reach full offshore team productivity within 90 days"
- "Client K clients average 5-year staff tenure vs. the 30–40% annual attrition across the broader Philippine IT-BPM sector"

**The "just right" test:**
- Too generic: "We offer marketing services" — could apply to any company
- Too narrow: "We provide 3-step consulting for 4-person dental practices in specific Sydney suburbs on Tuesdays" — niche is unprofitable
- Just right: "We help high-ticket home service companies increase booked jobs through local SEO and Google Ads" — specific audience, specific outcome, specific method

## 5. Evidence Standards

Every factual claim requires one of:
- A named source with year ("ABS 2024", "Hays Salary Guide FY25/26")
- A named institution ("Reserve Bank of Australia")
- A cited case study with attribution
- Explicit flagging as opinion ("In our view..." or "Some analysts argue...")

Unattributed statistics are a red flag. When in doubt, flag for fact-check rather than include.

---

## 6. No Filler

Every paragraph must either advance the reader's understanding or advance the argument. Cut anything that doesn't.

**Filler patterns to eliminate:**
- Summary paragraphs that restate what was just said
- Transition paragraphs that bridge without adding content
- "In conclusion, as we have seen..."
- "In today's rapidly changing world..." / "Now more than ever..."
- Rhetorical questions that aren't answered
- Hedge stacks: "It's worth noting that it may be possible that in some cases..."

**Test:** Remove the paragraph. Does the reader lose anything? If no, cut it.

## 7. Content Quality — The SERP Standard

Every piece must pass three content tests:

**Table stakes:** Covers everything the top-ranking competitors cover. A reader who has read the benchmark article should find everything they expect — plus more.

**Gap fill:** Identifies at least one topic, angle, or question that competitors miss or handle weakly. The research brief surfaces these gaps explicitly.

**Novel addition:** Adds at least one thing above what the SERP offers. Options:
- An angle or framing competitors haven't used
- A comparison framework (table, matrix, decision tree) that makes a decision easier
- A counterintuitive point or common misconception addressed directly
- Client-specific insight, case study, or proprietary data
- Synthesis connecting two ideas others treat separately

If the entire piece could be assembled by reading the top 5 search results — it is not good enough.

## 8. Structure — MECE

**Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive:**
- Each section covers distinct ground — no overlap between H2s
- All H2s together cover the full topic without gaps
- Test: can you remove any H2 without the reader missing something important? If yes, it either needs content added or it shouldn't exist.

**Reader-first ordering:**
- The reader's most important question is answered first
- Definition and background come after the answer, not before
- Supporting detail follows the headline claim within each section

---

## 9. E-E-A-T Signals

**Experience:** Written as if by someone who has dealt with this in practice. Specific scenarios, real-world caveats, practitioner language — not textbook descriptions.

**Expertise:** Correct technical terminology. Acknowledges nuance and edge cases. Doesn't oversimplify.

**Authoritativeness:** Cites primary sources. Links to original research. Doesn't cite other content aggregators as sources.

**Trustworthiness:** Balanced treatment of risks and benefits. Discloses limitations. Does not overclaim. Distinguishes general information from personalised advice where legally required.

## 10. Depth & Length

**Length is determined by intent, not a word count target.**

Skyscraper content (maximising length) is an outdated proxy for quality. The right length is the minimum needed to fully serve the search intent.

| Intent type | Length guidance |
|---|---|
| Specific question (how-to, what is) | 800–1,500 words. Answer directly. No padding. |
| Comparison / decision guide | 1,500–2,500 words. Cover all options. Include comparison framework. |
| Comprehensive guide / pillar | 2,500–4,000 words. MECE coverage. H3 sub-sections. |
| Deep technical or regulatory topic | As long as required. No artificial ceiling. |

**Depth calibration by keyword difficulty:**

| KD | Audience | Depth required |
|---|---|---|
| 0–30 | General / beginner | Comprehensive but accessible. No assumed knowledge. |
| 30–60 | Informed / researching | Assume basic familiarity. Add nuance, comparisons, edge cases. |
| 60+ | Expert | Demonstrate genuine expertise. Original analysis. Primary sources. High information density. |

## 11. Critique Scoring Rubric

Score each dimension 1–3:

| Dimension | 1 — Fails | 2 — Acceptable | 3 — Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hook quality** | Summary or generic opener | Has a hook but technique is weak | Named technique executed well, compels reading |
| **H2 quality** | Label-like, no benefit signal | Some benefit orientation | Benefit-focused, action-oriented, MECE |
| **Writing quality** | Weasel words, passive, vague | Mostly specific, some filler language | Dense, active, specific throughout |
| **B2B specificity** | Generic claims throughout | Some quantified outcomes | All major claims meet specificity test |
| **Evidence** | No sources cited | Some sourced, some not | All factual claims sourced |
| **No filler** | Multiple filler paragraphs | Occasional filler | Tight throughout |
| **Originality** | Aggregates existing content | One original element | Clear differentiation — beats SERP |
| **Structure (MECE)** | Overlapping or incomplete sections | Logical but imperfect | Mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive |
| **E-E-A-T** | Reads like AI aggregation | Some expertise signals | Demonstrates genuine authority |
| **Benchmark comparison** | Does not beat named benchmark | Comparable to benchmark | Demonstrably exceeds benchmark |
| **Narrative craft** | Robotic or stilted | Readable but flat | Engaging, varied rhythm, human |
| **Depth / length** | Superficial or padded | Matches intent | Exceeds competitor depth at appropriate length |

**Minimum to pass:** No 1s. Average 2+. At least three 3s.

Any dimension scoring 1 triggers the Gap Research → Rewrite loop. The gap research brief must specifically address the failing dimension — not just general improvement.
