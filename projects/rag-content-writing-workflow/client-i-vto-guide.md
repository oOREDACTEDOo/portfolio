# Client I — V2O (Content Brief) Writing Guide

The V2O field is your editorial directive for a single piece. It overrides and focuses the AI's defaults.
Write it in 100–200 words max. Be specific and restrictive — tell it what to do AND what not to do.

---

## Template

```
AUDIENCE: [Year 12 / Parent / Regional student / Career changer]
GOAL: [The one thing this piece needs to achieve]
ANGLE: [The specific hook or frame for this piece]
INCLUDE: [1–3 specific points, examples, or quotes to use]
AVOID: [What not to cover, what tone to avoid]
TONE NOTE: [Any specific voice reminder for this piece]
```

## Worked Examples

---

### Example 1 — EBM awareness piece for Year 12

```
AUDIENCE: Year 12 students who've seen EBM in the course list but don't understand it.
GOAL: Educate them on what EBM actually is without overwhelming them.
ANGLE: "The glue that keeps everything working" — EBM is the course for people
who want to make creative projects happen, not just perform in them.
INCLUDE:
- Clare's quote: "The easiest way to find out what a producer does is to try
  and do anything without one."
- One career mobility example: the A&R manager who became a merch/tour
  logistics person wearing multiple hats
- Mention the double degree option briefly
AVOID: Don't list every career role. Don't limit it to music festivals.
Don't use hustle culture language.
TONE NOTE: Curious and reassuring. "You don't need to know exactly what you
want yet — EBM shows you what's possible."
```

---

### Example 2 — EBM piece targeting parents

```
AUDIENCE: Parents who are skeptical about a "creative business degree."
GOAL: Reframe EBM as practical, transferable business training — just in a
creative context.
ANGLE: Compare directly to a traditional business degree. Client I's version is
identical rigour, but every case study and hypothetical is about the industry
their kid actually wants to work in.
INCLUDE:
- Clare's quote: "You're not going to be given a marketing hypothetical about
  selling pharmaceuticals. You're going to be given one about launching an album."
- Clare's own career path: wedding logistics → festival production → TV producer
  (shows real-world transferability and stability)
- Mention professional facilities and industry-led teaching briefly
AVOID: Don't use "passion" or "dream" language — parents want viability,
not inspiration. Don't oversell the glamour.
TONE NOTE: Calm, informative, confident. Treat the parent as intelligent.
```

---

### Example 3 — Regional student piece (general brand)

```
AUDIENCE: Students outside major cities considering relocating to study.
GOAL: Make relocation feel manageable and worth it, not scary.
ANGLE: "Moving to the big smoke" — normalise the journey by showing real
students who did it and what their life actually looks like.
INCLUDE:
- Specific catchment areas: Gold Coast → Brisbane, Orange → Sydney,
  Geelong → Melbourne (use whichever is most relevant to the campus)
- Practical detail: living arrangements, commuting options, support systems
- One real student story if available (Maddie from regional relocation UGC
  is the reference example)
AVOID: Don't make it sound like a big life decision — make it sound like
a logical next step. Don't be vague. Specifics build confidence.
TONE NOTE: Warm and practical. Peer-to-peer energy, not institutional.
```

### Example 4 — General Client I brand piece for Year 12 (ATAR anxiety)

```
AUDIENCE: Year 12 students in the middle of exam season who feel like
their ATAR determines their future.
GOAL: Reduce exam anxiety and open their mind to a creative pathway.
ANGLE: Your ATAR is one door. This is a different door entirely.
INCLUDE:
- No ATAR requirement — portfolio and passion over test scores
- "You don't need to be a gun guitarist" — position finding your niche
  as part of the experience, not a prerequisite
- Hands-on from day one — contrast with traditional university theory-first
AVOID: Don't mention ATAR more than once — this piece isn't about ATARs,
it's about possibility. Don't use the word "journey."
TONE NOTE: "Hot and smart art kid" energy. Fun, a little irreverent.
This is not a law degree brochure.
```

## Rules for Writing a Good V2O

1. **Pick one audience.** Don't try to speak to Year 12s and parents in the
   same piece.

2. **Pick one angle.** If you find yourself writing two goals, you have two
   pieces.

3. **Name specific examples.** "Use Clare's quote about pharmaceuticals" is
   useful. "Use relevant expert insights" is not.

4. **AVOID is as important as INCLUDE.** Telling the AI what NOT to do
   prevents it from trying to cover everything.

5. **Don't paste the whole strategy document.** That's what causes the
   "trying to achieve everything" problem. Distil it to what matters
   for this one piece.

6. **Brand pillars are background, not foreground.** The AI already knows
   Client I's voice and differentiators. The V2O is for what's specific
   to this piece — not a repeat of everything in the brand guide.
